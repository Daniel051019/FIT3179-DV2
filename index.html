01 / The field you choose

Different disciplines, different starting points

In 2025, dentistry reported a median full-time salary of $103,100, while pharmacy reported $62,600. These are study-area outcomes: a degree does not guarantee a particular job or salary.

Read the benchmark

Computing starts above the national median

Computing and information systems graduates reported a median of $80,000, $3,000 above the national median. Engineering and teacher education were also above it.

A higher salary only describes those in full-time work. The next chapter brings the graduates still seeking that kind of work into the picture.

02

02 / Beyond the salary headline

Good pay and full-time work do not always go together

Computing’s $80,000 median came with a 65.3% full-time employment rate. Pharmacy’s lower salary coincided with a 96.4% full-time employment rate. Comparing both measures gives a fuller picture of the start of working life.

A change in 2025: QILT changed how it defines the labour force, excluding unemployed graduates who were only passively seeking work. Full-time and overall employment rates are not directly comparable with earlier years. The page compares employment rates within 2025 rather than treating the 2024–25 change as a labour-market improvement.

03 / Who starts where?

The pay gap is visible within study areas

Male domestic undergraduates reported a national median of $79,000, compared with $75,300 for female graduates. Some study areas show the reverse pattern: psychology’s female median was $5,000 higher.

Read these as group differences

These gaps compare groups; they do not establish causation. Occupation, working hours and prior experience may affect salaries. These charts do not adjust for those factors. They do not compare equal pay for identical jobs.

These are descriptive differences between groups. They do not isolate equal pay for the same job, hours beyond the full-time threshold, occupation, prior experience or other characteristics. Published gender categories and suppression are preserved.

04 / The changing value of a first salary

Pay rose by 33%. Purchasing power rose much less.

The national median increased from $57,900 in 2016 to $77,000 in 2025. After adjusting with a consistent June-quarter CPI measure, the gain was about 1.9%. A larger salary number does not translate directly into the same increase in spending power.

Inflation adjustment uses the all-groups CPI weighted average of eight capital cities, June quarter of each year, rebased to 2025. This provides a consistent annual approximation to the graduate survey period, which ends in May. It does not measure each graduate’s personal expenses or local cost of living.

05 / The labour markets graduates enter

Place adds context to the national picture

Graduates enter different regional labour markets. These three maps describe the wider workforce and resident qualification base. They do not show state-level graduate starting salaries.

Count and concentration

A large base is different from a high share

NSW has the largest estimated number of residents with a bachelor degree as their highest qualification. The ACT has the highest proportion, 26.5%.

The equal-size tiles make small territories visible. They preserve approximate regional relationships while deliberately replacing land area with equal space.

These maps cannot establish where an individual graduate would earn most or whether a region’s higher pay comes from its mix of jobs.

What to take away

Read the salary alongside the opportunity.

Study area shapes the first salary, but full-time employment is uneven and gender differences remain within disciplines. Over time, much of the increase in pay has matched rising prices. For students considering work in Australia, the useful question is how salary, access to work and purchasing power fit together.
